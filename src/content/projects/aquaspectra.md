## The project

[AquaMesh](https://aquamesh.ai/) is a La Jolla company that makes optical water-quality sensors for coastal and industrial water. Its in-water probe, [AquaSpectra](https://aquamesh.ai/#aquaspectra), puts a UV/visible spectrometer, a fluorometer and a turbidity sensor in one housing, so a single instrument can measure things like nitrate, chlorophyll, dissolved organic matter (fDOM) and turbidity. AquaMesh is validating it with the [Coastal Ocean Observation Lab (COOL Lab)](https://coollab.ucsd.edu/) at Scripps Institution of Oceanography, on the Scripps Pier, which gets constant waves, changing salinity and a lot of biofouling.

## My part

I'm on a research project between AquaMesh and the COOL Lab to get the sensor ready for research use. That means the remaining software interface, the calibration workflow and tuning the sensor across marine conditions. Day to day, I review the probe and hub firmware and the front-end software, run calibration and test workflows, look at how the sensor behaves, and write up what I find and what I'd change.
